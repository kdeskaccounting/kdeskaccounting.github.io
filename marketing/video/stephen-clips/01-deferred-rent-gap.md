If you adopted ASC 842 and you still have a deferred rent liability on the balance sheet, / something went wrong at transition.

Here's the thing nobody says out loud. / Deferred rent didn't disappear. / It moved.

Take a 36-month office lease. One free month, three percent escalations. / Under the old rules, the straight-line rent versus what you actually paid builds up a deferred rent balance. / At month 24 it's three thousand five hundred and two dollars and sixty-seven cents.

Under ASC 842, that account is gone. / But look at the lease liability, and look at the right-of-use asset, at the same date. / The difference between them? / Three thousand five hundred and two dollars and sixty-seven cents. / To the penny.

Deferred rent is the gap between the liability and the ROU asset. / That's why the asset is always a little smaller than the liability on an operating lease, and that's the answer when an auditor asks.

I put the whole schedule, both sets of entries, and the transition entry in a post. / Link's in the comments.
