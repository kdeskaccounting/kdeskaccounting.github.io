**The situation**

A 36-month office lease starts January 1. Rent is $5,000, paid at the end of each month. There are
no lease incentives and no initial direct costs, and your incremental borrowing rate is 6%.

**At commencement**

The lease liability is the present value of 36 payments of $5,000 at 0.5% a month: $164,355.08.
With nothing to add or subtract, the right-of-use asset starts at the same amount.

```
Dr  Right-of-use asset        164,355.08
    Cr  Lease liability               164,355.08
```

**January 31**

Interest on the liability is $164,355.08 times 0.5%, or $821.78. The payment is $5,000, so the
liability falls by $4,178.22.

An operating lease shows one straight-line lease cost in the income statement, $5,000 here. So the
right-of-use asset comes down by what is left after interest: $5,000 less $821.78, or $4,178.22.

```
Dr  Lease expense               5,000.00
Dr  Lease liability             4,178.22
    Cr  Cash                            5,000.00
    Cr  Right-of-use asset              4,178.22
```

**The tie-out**

With level payments made at month-end and no incentives or direct costs, the right-of-use asset and
the lease liability stay equal every month. Both are $160,176.86 at January 31. If they drift apart
on a lease like this one, something was posted to the wrong side.

The standard: ASC 842-20-25-6, a single lease cost recognized on a straight-line basis.
