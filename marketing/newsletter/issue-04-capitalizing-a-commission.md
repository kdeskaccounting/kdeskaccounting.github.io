**The situation**

A rep closes a new customer in January and earns an $18,000 commission. The contract runs one year,
and renewals pay no commission. The commission would not have been paid without the contract, and
you expect to recover it. You estimate the customer relationship will last three years.

**January: capitalize it**

```
Dr  Deferred commissions (asset)      18,000
    Cr  Accrued commissions payable           18,000
```

When payroll pays the rep, the payable clears against cash.

**Each month: amortize it**

$18,000 over 36 months is $500 a month, starting in January.

```
Dr  Commission expense (amortization)    500
    Cr  Deferred commissions                    500
```

**Why three years and not the contract term**

The asset is amortized as the related services transfer to the customer. That includes renewals you
expect, when the commission paid on a renewal is not commensurate with the one paid on the original
contract. Many software companies land near the expected customer life for that reason. It is a
judgment, and auditors ask to see how you made it.

**The tie-out**

At December 31 the asset is $18,000 less twelve months at $500, or $12,000. Many companies present
$6,000 of it as current and $6,000 as noncurrent. The rollforward reads beginning balance, plus capitalized, less amortization,
equals ending, and the ending balance totals by contract in the subledger.

The standard: ASC 340-40-25-1 for capitalizing, 340-40-35-1 for amortizing, and the practical
expedient in 340-40-25-4 when the amortization period would be one year or less.
