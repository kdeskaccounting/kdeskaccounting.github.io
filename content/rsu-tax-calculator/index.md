faq:
  - q: "Why do I owe tax on RSUs in April if my employer already withheld?"
    a: "Because RSU income is 'supplemental wages', most employers withhold a flat 22% federal rate on it (37% once supplemental wages exceed $1 million in a year). RSU income is taxed as ordinary income at your marginal rate, which is often 24%, 32%, 35% or 37% by the time vests are stacked on top of salary. The difference between the flat rate withheld and the marginal rate owed is the April shortfall."
  - q: "Is RSU withholding 22% or 37%?"
    a: "The IRS optional flat rate on supplemental wages is 22%. Once an employee's supplemental wages exceed $1,000,000 in a calendar year, the excess must be withheld at 37% (IRS Publication 15, 2026)."
  - q: "Does sell-to-cover mean my RSU taxes are paid?"
    a: "Sell-to-cover funds the withholding your employer is required to take — typically the 22% flat rate plus Social Security and Medicare — not the tax you ultimately owe. If your marginal rate is higher than 22%, sell-to-cover leaves the same gap as any other withholding method."
  - q: "What is the safe harbor for underpayment penalties?"
    a: "Under the federal estimated-tax rules, an underpayment penalty is generally avoided when withholding plus timely estimated payments reach at least 90% of the current year's tax, or 100% of the prior year's tax (110% if prior-year adjusted gross income was over $150,000). Withholding counts as paid evenly through the year unless you show when it was actually withheld; estimated payments are due April 15, June 15, September 15 and January 15, and the penalty is computed like interest on each shortfall. There is no penalty when the balance due after withholding is under $1,000."
  - q: "Are RSUs taxed as ordinary income?"
    a: "Yes. The fair market value of shares on the vest date is ordinary compensation income, reported on Form W-2 and subject to federal income tax, Social Security (up to the wage base, $184,500 for 2026) and Medicare, including the 0.9% Additional Medicare Tax above the applicable threshold. Any later gain or loss on the shares is a capital gain or loss measured from the vest-date value."
